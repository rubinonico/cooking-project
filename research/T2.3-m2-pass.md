## Verdict

**The overlap-driven week survived the M2 check.** Every dish scores above its cuisine analogue's p90. All but one internal pair are moderate or better. M2 did NOT find a dish that the overlap selector should have rejected.

**The one signal M2 found: garam_masala + cream returns "low" inside Butter Chicken.** This is a genuine finding — Epicure does not capture the tempering dynamic where fat mellows spice. But it is not a dish failure: the pair works in the real recipe, and every other pair in the dish is moderate or better. **Flag the gap; do not reject the dish.**

**Limitation — what this check did NOT verify:**
- It could not check whether two Indian dishes in one week is monotonous. That is a variety question, not a plausibility question.
- It could not check whether the week reads as food someone would actually cook on a Tuesday. That is a human judgement.
- It did not verify prep-load balance — Barrie's own scorecard needs her paywalled rubric to calibrate.

## What this means for the project

**If M2 finds nothing wrong with a purely overlap-maximised week (one flagged pair excepted), the engine adds little discrimination at the dish level.** The real value of culture-embedding may be upstream — in ingredient selection or substitution — rather than in post-hoc dish verification. That finding is worth stating plainly: M2 confirmed what the overlap selector picked, which means overlap alone may be a sufficient filter for plausible dishes in these three cuisines. The harder test would be a week that includes cuisines with weaker Epicure analogues (e.g. thai → Southeast_Asian) or dishes from opposite ends of the embedding space.
